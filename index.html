Bundelkhand Wedding Rituals: A 5-Day Celebration of Love, Folk Songs & Tradition

A look at how traditional weddings unfold in our region—from Lagun Likhai and Hardikot to Mandap Chhadna, Bhanwar, and Bidai.

By Bhaskar Thakur · 5 min read · Bundelkhand Heritage
Bundelkhand has wedding rituals that are full of folk songs, emotions and deep traditions. Unlike big city weddings, a Bundelkhandi Shaadi is not a one-day affair — it is celebrated for 4-5 days with the whole village as family.

To understand the soul of a traditional Bundelkhandi wedding, one must step away from modern commercial banquet halls and enter the village courtyard. Here, marriages remain deeply anchored in kinship, ancestral soil, and oral folklore. For five continuous days, the courtyard transforms into a sanctified space where every aunt, brother, neighbor, and elder has a defined sacred duty.

Across these five days, rituals flow seamlessly into one another—from the auspicious writing of the first horoscope letter to the joyous rubbing of turmeric, the emotional gathering of earth for the cooking fire, the playful teasing of the groom's procession, and the sacred circumambulations around living fire. Below is how the matrimonial journey authentically unfolds across each day.

Day 1: Lagun Likhai and Tika / Faldaan

Fixing the Auspicious Dates & Shagun

The celebrations kick off with Lagun Likhai. Family members and elders sit together with Pandit ji, who opens the panchang (calendar) to finalize the Shubh Muhurat—the exact auspicious times for each ceremony. Once the dates are set, Pandit ji writes the ceremonial Lagun Patrika using haldi and roli. This yellow wedding letter is formally sent over to the groom's house along with coconuts, sweets, and shagun.

Right after that comes Tika (or Faldaan). The bride's father, brothers, and close relatives travel to the groom's home with brass thalis filled with fresh seasonal fruits, homemade sweets, clothes, and cash shagun. The bride's father applies a vermilion tilak on the groom's forehead. It's an emotional and happy moment that officially seals the bond between both sides in front of relatives and neighbors.

"लगुन आई रे सखी, मंगल गाओ री... आजु हमारे द्वारे आनंद छायो री!"

Day 2: Tel, Haldi and Mehndi

Laughter, Songs & Courtyard Traditions

The second day belongs completely to the women of the family. The courtyard fills up with the smell of raw mustard oil, freshly ground turmeric roots, and cool henna. It's a day full of fun, jokes, and singing:

1. The Tel Ritual

In the morning, the mother and elder women start the Tel ritual. Using soft grass (doob), they dip mustard oil and haldi and touch it gently to the bride's feet, knees, shoulders, and forehead five or seven times. It's an old traditional custom meant to bless the bride with good health, glowing skin, and peace of mind before the big day.

2. Hardikot (Haldi)

In Bundelkhand, we don't just call it haldi—we call it Hardikot. Cousins, sisters, and bhabhis take bowls of fresh yellow paste made on the sil-batta (grinding stone) and smear it all over the bride with lots of laughter. Nobody gets spared; the cousins end up chasing each other with yellow hands while singing playful teasing songs.

3. Mehndi and All-Night Sangeet

By late afternoon, the mehndi artists and cousins sit with the bride to apply dark green henna on her hands and feet. As night sets in, women and girls from the whole neighborhood pull out charpais (cots) in the open courtyard. Someone starts beating the dholak with a spoon, and everyone joins in singing our famous Rai and Dadra folk songs late into the night.

"हरदी चढ़े रे लाडली, नखराले नैनन में... सखियां गावें राई-दादरा, सजे दुल्हन अंगना में!"

Day 3: Mandap Chhadna and Devi Pujan

Setting Up the Mandap & Family Blessings

Day three is when wedding preparations go into full swing at home. This is the day where maternal uncles and neighborhood aunts take center stage, performing age-old customs that bind the family together:

1. Raising the Mandap

The wedding canopy (mandap) is set up in the courtyard using bamboo and fresh mango branches. By tradition, it is the maternal uncle (Mama ji) who brings the very first branch to put up the structure. It's an essential ritual showing the love, support, and responsibility the mother's family holds for the bride.

2. Matkor and Chulha Pujan

One of the most unique Bundelkhandi traditions happens in the afternoon: Matkor. Women from the neighborhood walk together carrying brass plates and a small spade, singing joyful songs on their way to dig clean soil from the field. They bring this earth back home to build and plaster the traditional mud stove (chulha) where the community wedding feast will be prepared. The mother performs a small puja at the chulha with prayers for her daughter's future home.

3. Ganesh Pujan and Kalash Sthapana

Pandit ji installs a brass water pot (kalash) topped with fresh mango leaves and a coconut, performing Ganesh Pujan. As the remover of obstacles, Lord Ganesha is asked to bless the house and keep everything safe and peaceful throughout the ceremony.

4. Kangna Bandhan

Before the day ends, Pandit ji ties a sacred thread called Kangna around the right wrist of the bride and the groom. This red-and-yellow thread holds a small iron ring, a dry turmeric knot, and mustard seeds. It acts as a protective shield against bad vibes or buri nazar until all the wedding rituals are completed.

Day 4: The Main Wedding Day — Baraat, Jaimala and Bhanwar

The Big Day: Baraat Arrival & The Seven Phere

This is the day everyone has been waiting for. From early morning till past midnight, the house is buzzing with guests, relatives, and neighbors:

Morning – Suraj Pujan: Early in the morning, the bride takes a bath and offers water to the rising Sun (Suraj Pujan) for strength and blessings. Soon after, sisters and cousins help dress her up in her bridal lehenga or red saree, matching traditional jewelry, and a decorated chunri.

Evening – Baraat Aagaman: Around dusk, the groom arrives with his baraat—accompanied by a brass band, lights, and dancing friends. When they reach the gate, our favorite local tradition begins: the "Baraat Swagat with Dadaiyaar". The women from the bride's family welcome the groom's side by singing hilarious, witty teasing songs (Dadaiyaar), roasting the groom's friends and relatives in good humor while everyone laughs.

1. Dwar Puja and Jaimala

At the doorstep, the bride's father and elders greet the groom with Dwar Puja, welcoming him respectfully into the household. Right after, the couple stands together for Jaimala, placing rose and marigold garlands around each other as family and friends shower them with flowers and cheer.

2. Kanyadaan and Bhanwar (Saat Phere)

Late at night, when the guests have eaten, the core wedding rites begin under the mandap. The bride's parents perform Kanyadaan with deep emotion. Then comes the most important part of all: Bhanwar (Saat Phere). The bride and groom take seven rounds around the sacred fire. What makes Bundelkhand weddings so special is that Pandit ji explains each of the seven promises directly in our everyday Bundeli language, so everyone sitting around understands the real meaning of each vow.

3. Sindoor Daan

Right after the phere, the groom applies bright red sindoor into the bride's hair parting. Dadi, Nani, and the elder women wipe tears from their eyes and sing traditional Suhag geet, blessing the newly married couple with long, happy lives together.

"भंवर पड़त हैं वेदी के आगे, सात वचन बंधे सात जन्म... मांग में सिंदूर सज गयो, जुग-जुग जियो जोड़ी धन्य!"

Day 5: Bidai and Kangna-Khel

An Emotional Farewell & Fun Games at the New Home

The fifth day brings a mixture of deep emotions—sadness as the bride leaves her childhood home, followed by warm celebration as she steps into her new family:

1. Bidai (The Farewell)

At dawn, the entire courtyard goes quiet and emotional. As the bride gets ready to leave, she performs the Khilona ritual: taking handfuls of puffed rice and tossing them backwards over her head into her mother's spread dupatta. It's a daughter's heartfelt prayer that her parents' home will always remain full of happiness, blessings, and prosperity even after she moves away.

2. Kangna and Jua-Jag at the Groom's House

When the bride reaches the groom's house, her mother-in-law welcomes her at the doorstep with Aarti and Mooh Dikhai. Later in the evening, all the cousins and in-laws crowd around the couple for Kangna Chhudai and Jua-Jag. They untie each other's wrist threads and compete to fish out a silver ring hidden inside a brass parat filled with milk, turmeric, and rose petals. It's a wonderful icebreaker that helps the bride feel relaxed and loved right from day one.

Why These Five Days Matter

In modern cities, weddings often feel like a rushed race—booking a banquet hall, posing for photos for three hours, and then packing up by midnight. But growing up around Bundelkhandi weddings teaches you what weddings used to be about: taking time to celebrate relationships.

When you see Mama bringing the first wood for the mandap, neighborhood chachis and dadis singing Rai geet until 2 AM, and the whole mohalla helping cook the feast, you realize that a wedding isn't just about two individuals. It is about a whole village wrapping a new couple in love, blessing, and lifelong support.
